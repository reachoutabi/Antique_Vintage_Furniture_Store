Identifying Authentic 18th-Century Georgian Mahogany Joinery

1. The Golden Age of Cuban Mahogany

Between 1720 and 1780, English furniture makers had unprecedented access to dense, dark old-growth mahogany imported from Cuba and San Domingo. Unlike modern plantation mahogany, 18th-century timber was heavy, close-grained, and naturally resistant to woodworm.

2. Hand-Cut Dovetail Characteristics

Machine-cut dovetails introduced in the late 19th century are perfectly uniform. Genuine 18th-century Georgian drawers exhibit narrow, hand-chiseled "pins" with slight variations in spacing and chisel score lines inside the drawer casing.

"Inspect the underside of drawer bottoms. True Georgian cabinetmakers used un-planed deal or oak boards displaying hand-pit saw marks."

3. Brassware & Lock Identification

Original George III brass handles were sand-cast and hand-chased. Look for cast brass backplates secured by hand-cut steel screws with off-center thread slots.
